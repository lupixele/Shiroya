## Q1. Describe the major theories of Agile Management and their relevance to Agile Software Development.

### 1. Introduction

**Agile Management** is an approach to managing software projects through flexibility, teamwork, continuous feedback and frequent delivery. It helps teams respond to changing customer needs instead of following a rigid plan throughout the project.

Agile management draws on ideas that emphasize people, collaboration, adaptation and continuous improvement.

### 2. Major theories / ideas relevant to Agile Management

**1. Empirical Process Control**
- Decisions are made using observation, experience and feedback.
- Work is inspected regularly to understand the current situation.
- Plans are adapted based on actual results.
- Relevance: Agile teams review progress during iterations and change their approach when necessary.

**2. Lean Thinking**
- Focuses on delivering value to the customer.
- Reduces waste, unnecessary work and delays.
- Encourages continuous improvement.
- Relevance: Agile teams prioritize useful features and avoid spending time on work that does not add value.

**3. Systems Thinking**
- Views the project as a connected system of people, processes and technology.
- A change in one part may affect other parts.
- Encourages teams to understand the complete workflow.
- Relevance: Agile teams coordinate development, testing, design and customer feedback rather than treating them as isolated activities.

**4. Self-Organization and Team Empowerment**
- Teams are given responsibility to organize their work.
- Members collaborate and decide how to complete tasks.
- This encourages ownership and faster decisions.
- Relevance: Agile teams plan and manage their work together.

**5. Continuous Improvement**
- Teams regularly examine what is working and what needs improvement.
- They make small changes to their process.
- Relevance: Retrospectives help Agile teams improve communication, quality and productivity after each iteration.

### 3. Relevance to Agile Software Development
- Helps teams adapt to changing requirements.
- Encourages customer collaboration.
- Improves communication and teamwork.
- Supports frequent delivery of working software.
- Helps identify problems early and improve the process continuously.

### 4. Example

A team developing an online shopping application reviews its work at the end of each iteration. Customer feedback shows that product search is difficult. The team changes its next iteration plan and improves the search feature. This demonstrates feedback-based management and continuous improvement.

### 5. Conclusion

Agile Management uses ideas such as empirical control, Lean thinking, systems thinking, self-organization and continuous improvement. These ideas help teams deliver customer value while adapting to change.

**Memory trick: E--L--S--S--C = "Every Learner Studies, Shares, Corrects."**
- **E** — Empirical control
- **L** — Lean thinking
- **S** — Systems thinking
- **S** — Self-organizing teams
- **C** — Continuous improvement

## Q2. Differentiate between Traditional and Agile Software Development Models with respect to planning, requirements, development, testing, and delivery.

### 1. Introduction

Traditional software development generally follows a plan-driven and sequential approach. Agile software development follows an iterative and incremental approach in which teams deliver software frequently and respond to feedback.

### 2. Differences

| Basis | Traditional Model | Agile Model |
|---|---|---|
| **Planning** | Detailed planning is usually done at the beginning | Planning is repeated during each iteration |
| **Requirements** | Requirements are expected to be defined early | Requirements can evolve with feedback |
| **Development** | Work generally moves through planned phases | Work is completed in short iterations |
| **Testing** | Testing is often performed after development | Testing is continuous throughout development |
| **Customer involvement** | Customer involvement may be concentrated at the beginning and end | Customer collaboration is frequent |
| **Changes** | Changes can be difficult and costly | Changes can be included in future iterations |
| **Delivery** | Often one major delivery at the end | Frequent delivery of working software |
| **Documentation** | Usually more detailed, plan-driven documentation | Documentation is useful and sufficient for the work |

### 3. Explanation

**Traditional model**
- The project scope and requirements are defined early.
- A detailed plan is prepared.
- Design and coding follow the plan.
- Testing is generally performed after implementation.
- The final product is delivered after the planned stages.

**Agile model**
- The project is divided into small iterations.
- The team plans a limited amount of work for each iteration.
- Design, coding and testing happen repeatedly.
- Customers review working software and provide feedback.
- New or changed requirements can be prioritized in later iterations.

### 4. Example

For an online shopping application:
- **Traditional:** Login, product search, cart, payment and reports may all be planned before development, with the main release at the end.
- **Agile:** Login and product browsing may be delivered first, followed by cart, payment and order tracking in later iterations.

### 5. Advantages of Agile over a rigid traditional approach
- Better response to changing requirements.
- Earlier delivery of useful features.
- Frequent customer feedback.
- Continuous testing and improvement.
- Greater visibility of project progress.

### 6. Conclusion

Traditional models are useful when requirements are stable and predictable. Agile is useful when requirements may change and regular customer feedback and frequent delivery are important.

**Memory trick: P--R--D--T--D = Planning, Requirements, Development, Testing, Delivery.**

Remember: **Traditional = Plan first, deliver later. Agile = Plan repeatedly, deliver frequently.**

## Q3. Discuss the four values of the Agile Manifesto and illustrate each value with a suitable example.

### 1. Introduction

The **Agile Manifesto** is a statement of values and principles that guides Agile software development. It was created to encourage flexible development, customer collaboration and frequent delivery of working software.

It contains four important values.

### 2. Four values of the Agile Manifesto

**1. Individuals and interactions over processes and tools**
- People and communication are more important than depending only on procedures or tools.
- Team members should discuss problems and work together.
- Example: Developers directly discuss a coding issue with the tester instead of depending only on lengthy email exchanges.

**2. Working software over comprehensive documentation**
- A working product is more valuable than producing excessive documentation.
- Documentation is still useful, but it should support development.
- Example: A team demonstrates a working login feature to the customer rather than only presenting a long design document.

**3. Customer collaboration over contract negotiation**
- Customers should participate throughout development.
- Their feedback helps the team understand changing needs.
- Example: A customer reviews a shopping application's cart feature and requests a quantity update option. The team considers it for the next iteration.

**4. Responding to change over following a plan**
- Teams should be ready to adapt when requirements change.
- A plan is useful, but it should not prevent necessary improvements.
- Example: A college requests an additional attendance report. The Agile team prioritizes and schedules the feature in a later iteration.

### 3. Importance of the four values
- Improves communication and teamwork.
- Focuses on useful working software.
- Increases customer satisfaction.
- Supports changing requirements.
- Encourages flexible and continuous development.

### 4. Conclusion

The four Agile Manifesto values emphasize people, working software, customer collaboration and responding to change. They guide teams toward flexible and customer-focused development.

**Memory trick: I--W--C--R = "I Work, Collaborate, Respond."**
- **I** — Individuals and interactions
- **W** — Working software
- **C** — Customer collaboration
- **R** — Responding to change

## Q4. Analyze the principles of the Agile Manifesto and their contribution to iterative and customer-focused development.

### 1. Introduction

The Agile Manifesto contains **12 principles** that guide teams in applying Agile values. These principles encourage frequent delivery, customer satisfaction, teamwork, technical excellence and continuous improvement.

### 2. Twelve principles of the Agile Manifesto

**1. Customer satisfaction through early and continuous delivery**
- Deliver useful software early and regularly.
- This allows customers to receive value sooner.

**2. Welcome changing requirements**
- Accept changes even during later development.
- This helps the product remain useful to customers.

**3. Deliver working software frequently**
- Release working software in short periods.
- This provides regular progress and feedback.

**4. Business people and developers work together**
- Maintain regular communication between business representatives and developers.
- This reduces misunderstandings.

**5. Build projects around motivated individuals**
- Provide support and trust to team members.
- Give them the environment and resources needed to work.

**6. Face-to-face conversation is effective**
- Direct communication helps explain problems quickly.
- It reduces confusion and delays.

**7. Working software is the primary measure of progress**
- Progress is judged by completed, functioning features.
- It is not measured only by documents or plans.

**8. Maintain a sustainable development pace**
- Teams should work at a pace they can maintain.
- Avoid continuous overwork and burnout.

**9. Continuous attention to technical excellence and good design**
- Good coding and design improve quality.
- They make future changes easier.

**10. Simplicity is essential**
- Avoid unnecessary work and complicated solutions.
- Build only what provides value.

**11. Self-organizing teams produce good designs**
- Teams organize tasks and make technical decisions.
- This encourages ownership and creativity.

**12. Regular reflection and adjustment**
- Teams review their performance regularly.
- They improve their working methods.

### 3. Contribution to iterative development
- Software is delivered in small, frequent increments.
- Feedback from each increment guides the next one.
- Testing and improvement happen continuously.
- Teams can adjust plans based on actual results.

### 4. Contribution to customer-focused development
- Customers receive useful software early.
- Customers can give feedback regularly.
- Changing requirements are accepted.
- Business people and developers collaborate.
- The team focuses on customer value.

### 5. Example

In a college attendance application, the team first delivers student login and attendance entry. Faculty members review it and request a monthly attendance report. The team adds this feature in a later iteration. This demonstrates early delivery, customer feedback and responding to change.

### 6. Conclusion

The 12 Agile principles support iterative development by encouraging frequent releases and continuous improvement. They support customer-focused development through collaboration, feedback and flexibility.

**Memory trick:** Remember the 12 principles in four groups of three:
- **Customer:** Satisfaction, Change, Frequent delivery.
- **Team:** Collaboration, Motivation, Direct communication.
- **Product:** Working software, Sustainable pace, Technical excellence.
- **Improvement:** Simplicity, Self-organization, Regular reflection.

Short recall code: **C--T--P--I.**

## Q5. Classify the major Agile methods and compare their key characteristics.

### 1. Introduction

Agile methods are approaches used to develop software iteratively and incrementally. They emphasize collaboration, customer feedback, working software and the ability to respond to change.

### 2. Major Agile methods

**1. Scrum**
- Organizes work into fixed-length iterations called Sprints.
- Uses roles such as Product Owner, Scrum Master and Developers.
- Uses Product Backlog and Sprint Backlog.
- Includes Sprint Planning, Daily Scrum, Sprint Review and Retrospective.

**2. Extreme Programming (XP)**
- Focuses strongly on software quality and technical practices.
- Uses pair programming and continuous integration.
- Encourages Test-Driven Development (TDD).
- Uses small releases and frequent customer feedback.

**3. Crystal**
- A family of Agile methods that can be adapted to project size and team needs.
- Emphasizes people, communication and teamwork.
- Encourages frequent delivery and reflection.
- Uses an approach suited to the project context.

**4. Feature Driven Development (FDD)**
- Organizes development around small, client-valued features.
- Uses feature lists and a feature-based development plan.
- Progress is tracked through completed features.
- Uses domain modeling and regular builds.

**5. Adaptive Software Development (ASD)**
- Designed for changing and uncertain environments.
- Uses the cycle: Speculate, Collaborate and Learn.
- Encourages adaptation and learning.
- Supports continuous feedback and improvement.

### 3. Comparison of Agile methods

---
| Method | Main Focus | Key Characteristics |
|---|---|---|
| **Scrum** | Project management | Sprints, roles, backlog and meetings |
| **XP** | Technical excellence | Pair programming, TDD and continuous integration |
| **Crystal** | People and communication | Adaptable methods and teamwork |
| **FDD** | Feature-based development | Domain model and feature lists |
| **ASD** | Adaptability and learning | Speculate, Collaborate, Learn |

### 4. Importance
- Different methods suit different project needs.
- Teams can select a method based on project size, complexity and requirements.
- These methods encourage customer involvement.
- They support incremental delivery and continuous improvement.

### 5. Conclusion

Scrum, XP, Crystal, FDD and ASD are important Agile methods. Each has a different focus, but all support flexible development, teamwork and delivery of useful software.

**Memory trick: S--X--C--F--A = "Smart Xperts Choose Features Adaptively."**
- **S** — Scrum
- **X** — XP
- **C** — Crystal
- **F** — FDD
- **A** — ASD

## Q6. Demonstrate how Agile Project Management supports team interactions, roles, and responsibilities.

### 1. Introduction

**Agile Project Management** is the process of managing software projects through short iterations, collaboration, regular feedback and continuous improvement. It supports communication among team members and helps them deliver working software frequently.

### 2. Important roles and responsibilities

**1. Product Owner**
- Represents customer and business needs.
- Maintains and prioritizes the Product Backlog.
- Explains requirements to the team.
- Ensures that development focuses on customer value.

**2. Scrum Master / Agile Facilitator**
- Helps the team follow Agile practices.
- Removes or helps resolve obstacles.
- Supports communication and collaboration.
- Helps the team improve its process.

**3. Development Team**
- Designs, codes and tests the software.
- Plans and completes the work in each iteration.
- Shares responsibility for delivering a working product.
- Communicates progress and problems.

**4. Customers and Stakeholders**
- Explain needs and expectations.
- Review working software.
- Give feedback and suggest changes.
- Help confirm whether the product provides value.

### 3. Team interaction practices

**1. Daily communication**
- Team members share progress and discuss obstacles.
- Problems can be identified quickly.

**2. Iteration planning**
- The team selects work for the next iteration.
- Tasks and responsibilities are understood.

**3. Review meetings**
- Completed software is demonstrated to stakeholders.
- Feedback is collected.

**4. Retrospective meetings**
- The team discusses what went well and what needs improvement.
- Action points are selected for the next iteration.

**5. Collaboration**
- Developers, testers and other team members work together.
- Knowledge is shared and issues are solved collectively.

### 4. How Agile Project Management supports the team
- Creates clear responsibilities.
- Improves communication.
- Encourages shared ownership.
- Helps remove obstacles.
- Provides regular visibility of progress.
- Supports quick decisions and adaptation.
- Encourages continuous improvement.

### 5. Example

In a college management software project, the Product Owner prioritizes attendance and marks features. The facilitator helps remove obstacles. Developers build and test the modules. Faculty members review the working features and provide feedback.

### 6. Conclusion

Agile Project Management supports effective teamwork by defining responsibilities, encouraging regular interaction and involving stakeholders. It helps the team deliver useful software through continuous collaboration.

**Memory trick: P--S--D--C = "Plan, Support, Develop, Collaborate."**
- **P** — Product Owner
- **S** — Scrum Master
- **D** — Development Team
- **C** — Customer / Stakeholders

For team interactions remember **P--D--R--R**: Planning, Daily communication, Review, Retrospective.

## Q7. Examine the importance of ethics, agility in design and testing, and Agile documentation within an Agile team.

### 1. Introduction

An Agile team must follow ethical practices, maintain flexible design and testing, and prepare useful documentation. These activities help the team build quality software while responding to customer needs and changes.

### 2. Ethics in an Agile team

Ethics means following professional values and behaving honestly and responsibly.

Important ethical practices include:

**1. Honesty and transparency**
- Report progress, problems and risks honestly.
- Do not hide defects or provide false status reports.

**2. Respect and teamwork**
- Respect the opinions and skills of other team members.
- Avoid discrimination and unfair treatment.

**3. Customer confidentiality**
- Protect customer information and private data.
- Do not share confidential information without permission.

**4. Responsibility and quality**
- Take responsibility for assigned work.
- Report defects and avoid knowingly delivering unsafe or poor-quality software.

### 3. Agility in Design

Agility in design means keeping software design flexible so that it can be improved when requirements change.

Important practices:
- Use simple and understandable designs.
- Apply suitable design principles such as SOLID.
- Avoid unnecessary complexity.
- Refactor code when needed.
- Design modules so that changes in one area have limited effect on others.

**Example:** A payment application uses separate payment modules so that another payment method can be added without rewriting the entire application.

### 4. Agility in Testing

Agility in testing means testing continuously during development rather than waiting until the end.

Important practices:
- Test each feature during the iteration.
- Use automated tests where suitable.
- Apply Test-Driven Development when appropriate.
- Perform regression testing after changes.
- Use customer acceptance tests to check requirements.

**Example:** Whenever a shopping cart feature changes, automated tests check that adding, removing and updating products still work.

### 5. Agile Documentation

Agile documentation means preparing documentation that is useful, clear and sufficient for development and maintenance.

Characteristics:
- Simple and easy to understand.
- Focused on important information.
- Updated when important changes occur.
- Supports communication among team members.
- Avoids unnecessary documentation that does not add value.

Examples include user stories, acceptance criteria, architecture notes, API documentation and test cases.

### 6. Importance
- Ethics builds trust and professional responsibility.
- Agile design makes software easier to change and maintain.
- Continuous testing helps detect defects early.
- Useful documentation supports teamwork and future maintenance.
- Together, these practices improve software quality and customer satisfaction.

### 7. Conclusion

Ethics, flexible design, continuous testing and useful documentation are important for a successful Agile team. They help the team work responsibly, adapt to changes and deliver quality software.

**Memory trick: E--D--T--D = "Ethics, Design, Testing, Documentation."**

Remember:
- Ethics = **Honesty**
- Design = **Flexibility**
- Testing =
**Continuously**
- Documentation = **Useful and simple**

## Q8. Design an Agile implementation plan for a retail software company migrating from Waterfall to Agile, covering Agile values, project management, team interaction, testing, and documentation.

### 1. Introduction

A retail software company may use the Waterfall Model to develop software through sequential phases. When customer needs and business requirements change frequently, the company may migrate to Agile to improve flexibility, collaboration and delivery speed.

An Agile implementation plan explains how the company can gradually introduce Agile practices.

### 2. Step 1: Adopt Agile values

The company should follow the four values of the Agile Manifesto:
- **Individuals and interactions:** Encourage direct communication among developers, testers, managers and business teams.
- **Working software:** Focus on delivering useful retail features instead of only producing documents.
- **Customer collaboration:** Involve retail users and business representatives regularly.
- **Responding to change:** Accept changing requirements and prioritize them in future iterations.

### 3. Step 2: Introduce Agile Project Management
- Select a pilot project, such as an online retail application.
- Create and prioritize a Product Backlog.
- Divide the work into short Sprints.
- Assign clear responsibilities.
- Conduct Sprint Planning, Daily Scrum, Sprint Review and Retrospective.
- Track completed work and remove obstacles.

### 4. Step 3: Improve team interaction
- Create a cross-functional team with developers, testers and designers.
- Encourage daily communication.
- Involve business representatives and customers.
- Share progress and problems openly.
- Conduct regular review and retrospective meetings.
- Encourage shared responsibility for the product.

### 5. Step 4: Adopt Agile testing
- Perform testing during every Sprint.
- Write acceptance criteria for user stories.
- Use unit and integration tests.
- Apply TDD where suitable.
- Automate repeated tests.
- Perform regression testing after changes.
- Conduct acceptance testing with retail users.

### 6. Step 5: Maintain Agile documentation
- Write clear user stories.
- Define acceptance criteria for features.
- Maintain a Product Backlog and Sprint Backlog.
- Prepare necessary design and architecture notes.
- Maintain test cases and technical documentation.
- Update important documents when the product changes.
- Avoid unnecessary paperwork.

### 7. Example: Retail Software Migration Plan

| Sprint | Main Work |
|:---:|---|
| **Sprint 1** | Customer login and registration |
| **Sprint 2** | Product catalogue and search |
| **Sprint 3** | Shopping cart |
| **Sprint 4** | Payment integration |
| **Sprint 5** | Order tracking and reports |

At the end of each Sprint, the company demonstrates the working features, collects feedback and adjusts the backlog.

### 8. Benefits of migration
- Faster delivery of useful features.
- Better response to changing customer needs.
- Improved team communication.
- Earlier identification of defects.
- Better customer involvement.
- Continuous improvement in software quality.

### 9. Conclusion

The retail company can migrate from Waterfall to Agile by adopting Agile values, introducing iterative project management, improving team interaction, testing continuously and maintaining useful documentation. A gradual migration through a pilot project reduces disruption and helps the organization learn Agile practices.

**Memory trick: V--P--T--T--D = "Values, Project Management, Team, Testing, Documentation."**
- **V** — Agile Values
- **P** — Project Management
- **T** — Team Interaction
- **T** — Testing
- **D** — Documentation

For the example, remember the retail flow: **Login → Catalogue → Cart → Payment → Tracking.**
