# 03. Mixture and Alligation

## 1. Theory and formulas

### 1.1 Weighted average
A mixture's concentration or average value depends on **both quantities and values**.

**Formula: final value = (q₁v₁ + q₂v₂)/(q₁+q₂)**.
For concentration, use percentages as fractions or as consistent percentage numbers.

### 1.2 Alligation
For low value `L`, target mean `M`, high value `H` (with `L < M < H`):

**Quantity of cheaper/lower : quantity of dearer/higher = (H−M) : (M−L)**.

This is a ratio of **amounts**, not a ratio of prices.

### 1.3 Adding water / pure substance
Amount of solute remains unchanged if plain water is added. **New concentration = original solute / new total mixture × 100**.

### 1.4 Profit and cost price in mixtures
If selling price is `SP` at profit `p%`, **cost price of mixture = SP/(1+p/100)**. Alligate using cost price, not selling price.

## 2. Questions and answers
**Q1 (PPT).** 30 students average 45 kg; girls average 37, boys 49. Number of boys?
**Answer:** Alligation girls:boys=`(49−45):(45−37)=4:8=1:2`. Boys=`30×2/3=20`.

**Q2 (PPT).** 20 L at 30% alcohol. Add 5 L of water. New percentage?
**Answer:** alcohol=`20×0.30=6 L`; total=25 L. **24%**.

**Q3 (PPT).** Mixture has 70% milk and another 60% milk, mixed 3:2. New milk %?
**Answer:** `(3×70+2×60)/5=66%`.

**Q4 (PPT).** 150 kg wheat has 10% inferior grain; add pure good grain until inferior grain is 5%.
**Answer:** inferior remains 15 kg; target total=`15/.05=300 kg`. **Add 150 kg**.

**Q5 (PPT).** 25% solution and 13% solution produce 17%; if first is 8 L, find second.
**Answer:** high:low=`(17−13):(25−17)=4:8=1:2`; second = **16 L**.

**Q6 (PPT).** Tea at ₹60 and ₹65/kg, sell mixture at ₹68.20/kg with 10% profit. Ratio of cheaper:dearer?
**Answer:** mixture CP=`68.20/1.1 = ₹62`. Ratio=`(65−62):(62−60)=3:2`.

**Q7 (PPT).** 60 L mixture, acid:base=2:1, add base to make 1:2.
**Answer:** acid=40, base=20. Need base=80; **add 60 L**.

## 3. Tricks and tips
1. In alligation, **cross-subtract** differences from target mean.
2. If adding water only, **solute stays constant**.
3. Check that the target concentration lies **between** the two ingredient concentrations.
4. Convert profit selling price to **cost price before mixing**.

## 4. Additional exam notes
The same weighted-average method applies to prices, class marks, population growth, sugar, milk, alcohol and profit percentages. Pure component is 100%; pure diluent is 0%.
