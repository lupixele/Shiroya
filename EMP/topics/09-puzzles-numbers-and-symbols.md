# 09. Puzzles, Numbers and Symbols

> **Study guide:** theory and formulas follow the supplied slides; worked problems labeled **PPT** are adapted from the deck, while those labeled **additional** are teaching examples. The separate slide-reference file preserves all available on-slide text and inserted images.

## 1. Theory and formulas

### 1.1 Puzzle workflow
Puzzles give constraints about an unknown arrangement or relationship. The PPT lists **floor puzzles, box stacking, days/months, ranking/ordering, blood relations, comparisons, selection/grouping, mixed conditions**, and numbers/symbol arrangements.

**Method: Read → organize slots → place fixed clues → apply relative clues → eliminate impossible layouts → answer.**

### 1.2 Floor and box puzzles
Write positions from top to bottom (or number them). 'Three boxes above G' fixes G at **fourth from the top**. 'One box between X and Y' means their positions differ by **2**, not 1.

### 1.3 Day/month puzzles
Write an ordered list (Monday–Sunday; January–December). Fill exact placements first. 'Immediately before' means neighboring slots; 'some time before' can have a gap.

### 1.4 Number and symbol sequences
For a mixed arrangement of letters, digits and symbols, write each element with a **1-based index**. Check whether a question asks for the element itself, the item adjacent to it, or the number of elements between two positions. Count only the categories specified.

### 1.5 Relations and ordering
Represent older/taller/faster as inequalities, family relations as a small labeled tree, and selection puzzles as a candidate table.

## 2. Questions and answers
**Q1 (PPT).** Seven people P,Q,R,S,T,U,V have exams Mon–Sun. R Thursday, exactly four between U and T, neither Sunday; S immediately before Q; P not Friday; U not after Q. How many exams after Q?
**Answer:** PPT layout `Mon U, Tue S, Wed Q, Thu R, Fri V, Sat T, Sun P`. After Wednesday = **4 people**.

**Q2 (PPT).** Seven boxes D,E,F,G,S,T,U stacked. Three above G; one between T and G; three between T and S; S above G; E immediately below S; F above D; U not adjacent T. Bottommost?
**Answer:** PPT layout top-to-bottom `U, S, E, G, F, T, D`; **D**.

**Q3 (PPT).** Exams of P,Q,R,S,T,U,V. S Tuesday; two people between S and P; T immediately before U; R immediately after Q; one person between U and Q. Number after Q?
**Answer:** PPT layout `Mon V, Tue S, Wed T, Thu U, Fri P, Sat Q, Sun R`; **1**.

**Q4 (PPT).** April–October, each colleague in a different month; constraints give `Apr G, May A, Jun D, Jul F, Aug C, Sep E, Oct B`. Who travelled in July?
**Answer:** **F**.

## 3. Tricks and tips
1. 'Exactly n between' means **distance n+1 positions**.
2. Start with clues that fix a slot precisely.
3. Separate **must** conditions from **cannot** conditions.
4. The final arrangement must satisfy **every** clue—not just the last one used.

## 4. Additional exam notes
**PPT typo:** A box puzzle mentions `P` in a condition and solution although `P` is absent from its listed seven boxes. See the source text in the numbered slide reference. Do not learn a logically inconsistent arrangement as a standard answer.
