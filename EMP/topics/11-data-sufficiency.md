# 11. Data Sufficiency

> **Study guide:** theory and formulas follow the supplied slides; worked problems labeled **PPT** are adapted from the deck, while those labeled **additional** are teaching examples. The separate slide-reference file preserves all available on-slide text and inserted images.

## 1. Theory and formulas

### 1.1 What data sufficiency asks
A question is followed by statements **I** and **II**. Decide whether enough information exists to give a **unique answer**—not necessarily what that answer is.

### 1.2 Five standard answer types
**A:** Statement I alone sufficient.
**B:** Statement II alone sufficient.
**C:** Either statement alone sufficient.
**D:** Both together sufficient; neither alone sufficient.
**E:** Even both together insufficient.

Always use the question's printed mapping if option labels differ.

### 1.3 Test each statement separately
Ask these in order: (1) Can I answer using I only? (2) II only? (3) If neither, can I answer using both? Do not leak a fact from II into your test of I.

### 1.4 Unique yes/no versus unique number
For a yes/no question, a definite **No** is sufficient too. For numerical questions, if two values remain possible, the information is insufficient.

## 2. Questions and answers
**Q1 (PPT).** Youngest among M,P,T,R,W? I: T younger than only P and W. II: M younger than T but older than R.
**Answer:** I alone leaves M and R unresolved. II alone leaves P,W unresolved. Together `P,W > T > M > R`; **D (both together)**.

**Q2 (PPT).** Number of Arun's daughters? I: four children. II: Bijli and Chandani are sisters of Dilawar, Arun's son.
**Answer:** Fourth child could be a boy or girl, so **E (even both insufficient)**.

**Q3 (PPT).** Tallest among A,B,C,D? I: C shorter than only B. II: D taller than only A.
**Answer:** I implies B taller than everyone. II does not identify the tallest. **A (I alone sufficient)**.

**Q4 (PPT).** Is F the granddaughter of B? I: B father of M, M sister of T, T mother of F. II: F is parent of S and V, and R brother of T.
**Answer:** F's gender remains unknown even when both are combined; cannot prove granddaughter. **E (both insufficient)**.

**Q5 (additional).** Is x even? I: x divisible by 4. II: x divisible by 6.
**Answer:** Either statement independently proves x even. **C**.

## 3. Tricks and tips
1. Test I and II **independently** before combining.
2. Find **two possible answers** to prove insufficiency.
3. If you can say a firm 'No', the statement can still be sufficient.
4. Do not mistake 'at least 2 daughters' for 'exactly 2 daughters'.

## 4. Additional exam notes
For age/height, draw inequality chains; for family questions, distinguish gender and generation; for mathematical questions, check whether multiple values satisfy a condition.
